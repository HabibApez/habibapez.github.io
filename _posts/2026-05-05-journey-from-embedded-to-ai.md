For more than eight years, my day-to-day work has lived in the world of embedded automotive systems: tight constraints, deterministic behavior, rigorous validation, and long product life cycles. That foundation shaped the way I solve problems.

What changed is not my engineering discipline. What changed is the frontier I want to contribute to.

## Why AI, Why Now

The automotive industry is moving toward systems where perception, prediction, and intelligent control are central. I realized that my experience in ADAS, radar, and safety-critical development can become even more impactful when combined with strong machine learning depth.

## What I Am Building

My transition plan is practical:

1. Build portfolio projects in computer vision, sensor fusion, and LLM-assisted engineering workflows.
2. Strengthen fundamentals in statistics, optimization, deep learning, and experimentation.
3. Align all work with safety-critical thinking so AI systems are not only smart but trustworthy.

Current implementations:

- [ADAS Object Detection]({{ '/projects/adas-object-detection/' | relative_url }})
- [Sensor Fusion with Deep Learning]({{ '/projects/sensor-fusion-dl/' | relative_url }})
- [CAN Bus Anomaly Detection]({{ '/projects/can-bus-anomaly-detection/' | relative_url }})
- [LLM Agent for Automotive Compliance]({{ '/projects/llm-automotive-compliance/' | relative_url }})

## What Stays Constant

Even as tools evolve, the principles remain:

- Clear requirements
- Traceable decisions
- Measurable performance
- Engineering accountability

This blog documents that journey as I prepare for graduate studies in AI in Canada and continue building at the intersection of automotive embedded systems and intelligent software.
